# Draft problem statement

Complete this in Week 1. Keep it provisional.

Use the same four questions from the lecture, then add the two that follow.

- **User:** who will act on the result?
Think about who would use the predictions or insights from this data. For example: "A marketing manager," "A hospital administrator," or "A logistics coordinator."
Example: A business operations manager who needs to decide where to allocate resources.
- **Decision:** what choice may change?
What action will the user take based on the model's output?
Example: Whether to approve a loan application, or which customers to target with a specific promotion.
- **Evidence:** what information is needed to decide?
What data or metrics are you using to make that decision?
Example: Historical customer transaction data, demographic information, and past purchasing behavior.
- **Consequence:** what happens if we are wrong?
What is the cost of a false positive (predicting yes when it's no) or a false negative (predicting no when it's yes)?
Example: If we incorrectly predict a customer will churn, we might waste money on retention offers. If we miss a churner, we lose their revenue.
- **Desired value:** what improves if this works?
What is the ultimate benefit? (e.g., saving money, saving time, increasing sales, improving health outcomes).
Example: A 10% increase in customer retention, or a reduction in wasted marketing spend by 20%.
- **One assumption that must be checked:**
I am assuming that the historical data accurately reflects future customer behavior, and that the data is not missing any critical values.
